Stage 1: the question, the case and your answers

Discovery question: a lookup table gives Apple one vector, the same in every sentence. In “Love Apple Phones” a reader knows Apple is a company. Where, in a system that owns only that table, is that knowledge?

Case: the nearest catalogue items to Apple are fruit. A developer replaces each token's vector with the plain average of the three token vectors. (a) What do the three vectors look like after the fix? (b) What happens in a ten-word query with one word about phones? (c) Which requirements must a proper fix meet?

What you answered

(a) “I need apple vector to go closer to phone and love in the space”

You said where Apple should end up. The case asked something else: what the developer's fix does to all three vectors. On this page you run the fix yourself and see.

The map

Five questions

    What the covered part must do

    • Per token. One shared blend made the three dots land on one point.
    • By content. Equal shares left Apple among the fruit, and a share tied to position changed when the same words were reordered.
    • Any length. With equal shares the useful word's share shrinks as the query grows.
    • Learned. You set the sliders by hand because you know what Apple means here. The system must find such shares itself, for every sentence.

    This is the specification of the covered area. The next sessions uncover it from the bottom up. Where the picture stops being true: the map has two dimensions and nine items; real embeddings have hundreds of dimensions.

    Worked example

    Rewrite Apple and Love, each with its own shares, and compare with the developer's average.

    Check 1

    A system computes one sentence vector and gives it to every token of “Love Apple Phones”. How many different vectors do the three tokens have afterwards?

    Check 2

    The same shopper types “Love Apple Phones” and then “Phones Love Apple”. Which scheme gives Phones the same share for rewriting Apple in both?
