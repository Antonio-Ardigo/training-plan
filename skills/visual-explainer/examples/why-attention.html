Lesson 1 · Why it matters

Why attention

A model reads a long text and must connect two words that are far apart. Two designs do this in very different ways. About 10 minutes, then three short checks.

    Two ways to read

    Recurrent reader (for example an LSTM)

    Reads one word at a time, in order. It keeps one memory and rewrites it at every word. What it knows about an early word is whatever that memory still holds.

    Attention reader

    Looks at all words at once. Each word asks a question, compares it with every other word, and reads directly from the ones that match.

    1 · How far the information travels

    The fact you need is some words back. Set how far. In the recurrent reader it must survive every rewrite on the way. In the attention reader it is one read away.

    The same count applies in reverse during training: the correction signal (the gradient) must travel back through the same number of steps. LSTMs were designed to lose less per step than older recurrent networks, but the number of steps stays.

    2 · How the right word is found

    The last word asks: "which word is a place?" Each word gets a score for how well it answers. The bars show the share of attention each word receives. The scores are toy numbers: 4 for a place, 0.8 for "in", 0 otherwise.

    Found by content. The share on "drawer" depends on what the word is. Where it stands plays no part.

    The price of that. After a shuffle the attention reader gives exactly the same answer, so by itself it cannot tell "dog bites man" from "man bites dog". Word order has to be added to the input as extra information. The recurrent reader gets order for free, because it reads in order.

    3 · What it costs

    Every word compares itself with every word. That is one score per pair: a square table.

    WordsScores (attention)Steps (recurrent)

    Double the text, four times the work. The recurrent reader only doubles. In exchange, the attention reader computes all its scores at the same time, while the recurrent reader must wait for each step before the next.

    Three checks

    Check 1. A fact is at word 12 and is needed at word 500. How many rewrites must it survive in the recurrent reader?

    Check 2. A text grows from 1,000 to 3,000 words. By what factor does the attention score table grow?

    Check 3. With no order information added, the word that answers the question is moved 100 words earlier. Does its share of attention change?
